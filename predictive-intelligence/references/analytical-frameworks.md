# Predictive Intelligence — Analytical Frameworks Reference

## The Six Structural Lenses

These frameworks are the analytical engine of every predictive assessment. Apply at least three to any grounding data point before projecting forward.

---

### 1. Chokepoint Analysis

**Question**: Where does control concentrate, and who holds it?

Every system — digital, physical, financial, political — has nodes where disproportionate control concentrates. Identify the chokepoint, and you identify the power dynamic.

**Application pattern**:
- Map the system's dependency chain
- Identify the narrowest point (fewest actors, highest concentration)
- Determine who controls that point and what leverage it gives them
- Assess whether the chokepoint is contested, defended, or unrecognized

**Examples**:
- TSMC holds 90%+ of sub-7nm chip fabrication (semiconductor chokepoint)
- 97% of intercontinental data transits subsea cables (communications chokepoint)
- Strait of Hormuz handles 20% of global oil (energy chokepoint)
- SWIFT processes 90%+ of cross-border financial messaging (financial chokepoint)

**Projection pattern**: Chokepoints attract both defense and attack. If a chokepoint is undefended, project that it will be exploited. If contested, project fragmentation (alternative routes, parallel systems).

---

### 2. Deployment-Governance Gap

**Question**: How far ahead is deployment compared to governance?

Technology deploys 5-10 years before governance frameworks catch up. This gap is the structural vulnerability in every domain.

**Application pattern**:
- Identify when the technology was first deployed operationally
- Identify when governance frameworks were proposed, enacted, and enforced
- Measure the gap in years
- Assess whether governance is accelerating or the deployment is outrunning it

**Examples**:
- Social media deployed ~2006, meaningful regulation still incomplete in 2026 (20-year gap)
- Autonomous weapons deployed in combat (Israel, 2024), LAWS treaty not yet agreed (open gap)
- NIST PQC standards finalized 2024, enterprise migration estimated 5-10 years (2029-2034)
- EU AI Act entered force 2024, full enforcement 2026, but AI deployment began ~2018 (6-8 year gap)

**Projection pattern**: The gap is exploited by first movers (states, companies) who deploy before rules constrain them. Governance catches up after a major incident forces action. Project the incident, not the legislation.

### 3. Interdependence Weaponization

**Question**: Is this link cooperative or coercive — and for whom?

Every node of globalization — cables, chips, energy routes, financial messaging, space assets, data flows — is simultaneously a cooperative link and a coercive lever. The question is whether the relationship is symmetrical.

**Application pattern**:
- Identify the interdependency (trade, data, energy, technology)
- Assess symmetry: who needs whom more?
- Identify scenarios where the link becomes a lever (sanctions, export controls, service denial)
- Determine whether alternatives exist or are being built

**Examples**:
- US semiconductor export controls weaponize China's chip dependency
- Russia's gas exports to Europe weaponized energy interdependence (then Europe diversified)
- SWIFT access used as sanctions lever against Russia/Iran
- China's rare earth dominance as leverage over EV/defense supply chains

**Projection pattern**: Asymmetric interdependencies are unstable. The dependent party will pursue alternatives (BRICS Pay, Chinese chip fabs, alternative cable routes). Project the timeline of alternative development, not the continuation of dependency.

---

### 4. Structural Fragmentation

**Question**: Along what pre-existing fault lines will this system fragment under stress?

Systems under stress don't break randomly. They fragment along pre-existing fault lines — geographic, cultural, institutional, technical.

**Application pattern**:
- Identify the system under stress
- Map its pre-existing fault lines (jurisdictional, cultural, technical, economic)
- Assess the stress being applied (conflict, regulation, competition, resource scarcity)
- Project which fault lines crack first

**Examples**:
- The global internet fragmenting along US-allied / China-aligned / non-aligned lines
- EU AI regulation vs. US permissiveness creating regulatory fault lines
- Five Eyes intelligence cooperation fracturing if the US omits climate from threat assessments
- Financial system fragmenting as BRICS builds parallel payment infrastructure

**Projection pattern**: Fragmentation is gradual, then sudden. The slow phase (alternative systems being built) is observable now. Project the trigger event that accelerates fragmentation.

---

### 5. Pre-Positioning Doctrine

**Question**: What capabilities have been deployed before the conflict that would activate them?

The most dangerous capability is the one installed before anyone knows it will be used. Pre-positioned assets — cyber, military, financial, legal — are deployed during peacetime for wartime activation.

**Application pattern**:
- Identify capabilities deployed with no immediate operational purpose
- Assess whether the deployment pattern suggests future activation
- Determine the trigger conditions for activation
- Project the timeline and context of activation

**Examples**:
- Volt Typhoon inside US utility OT networks for 300 days (pre-positioned for Taiwan crisis)
- Iran's Electronic Operations Room built 2024-2025, activated within 24 hours of Epic Fury
- China's cable-cutting vessel capable of 4,000m depth (pre-positioned for Taiwan scenario)
- Russia's Pravda network poisoning AI training data (pre-positioned for epistemic warfare)

**Projection pattern**: Pre-positioned capabilities are revealed by their activation context. If the capability only makes sense in a specific crisis scenario, project that the crisis is being planned for.

---

### 6. Cascading Failure

**Question**: If this node fails, what other systems fail with it?

Infrastructure planned as discrete assets behaves as interconnected networks under stress. Failure at one node cascades through dependencies that weren't visible during normal operations.

**Application pattern**:
- Identify the node under stress or attack
- Map its downstream dependencies (what relies on it?)
- Map its upstream dependencies (what does it rely on?)
- Assess whether the cascade is linear (A→B→C) or exponential (A→B+C+D, each triggering further failures)

**Examples**:
- Hormuz closure → oil spike + helium shortage + LNG disruption → semiconductor production + Taiwan energy + defense industrial base
- AWS data center strike → banking + payments + delivery apps + AI services (UAE/Bahrain)
- Subsea cable cut → internet connectivity + financial transactions + military communications
- Grid cyberattack → power + water treatment + hospital systems + communications

**Projection pattern**: Cascading failures reveal hidden dependencies. After each cascade, project that the revealed dependencies will be (a) studied, (b) partially mitigated, but (c) replaced by new hidden dependencies as systems evolve.

## Combining Frameworks

The strongest assessments apply 3+ frameworks to the same grounding data. Example:

**Grounding**: Rogue communication devices found in Chinese solar inverters (May 2025)

- **Chokepoint Analysis**: 70% of solar components are Chinese-manufactured — supply chain chokepoint
- **Deployment-Governance Gap**: Millions of inverters installed before any inspection regime existed
- **Pre-Positioning Doctrine**: Hidden communication modules have no legitimate purpose — pre-positioned for activation
- **Cascading Failure**: Compromised inverters → grid instability → hospital/water/communications failure

**Combined projection**: By 2035, the renewable energy grid will be the most consequential unsecured attack surface in Western nations, with Chinese-manufactured components providing pre-positioned access that no existing inspection regime can retroactively address.
