# Predictive Intelligence — Structural Forecasting Skill

## Purpose

Generate authoritative, forward-looking intelligence assessments using DSI's structural forecasting method: disciplined identification of forces already in motion. The future isn't predicted — it's observed early.

This skill produces assessments that start from verified data points and trace structural trajectories. No speculation. No ideology. No punditry. Forces, incentives, and constraints — traced to their logical conclusions.

## When to Use This Skill

- Generating forward-looking article ideas for DSI, GISI, or ARIA
- Producing strategic assessments for clients or publications
- Scenario planning grounded in current intelligence
- Identifying emerging threats before they materialize
- Projecting geopolitical, technological, or security trajectories
- Building predictive briefings from open-source intelligence

## Core Method: The Three-Step Analytical Structure

Every assessment follows this structure without exception:

### Step 1: Grounding
A specific, verified data point from the present — a number, an event, a policy decision, a technical disclosure. Not an opinion. Not a trend. A fact.

**What qualifies as grounding data:**
- A specific number with a source (e.g., "2.63 million daily cyberattacks on Taiwan — ROC NSB")
- A dated event (e.g., "December 2025: Sandworm deployed DynoWiper against Polish energy infrastructure")
- A policy decision with a document reference (e.g., "January 2026: EU Commission proposed NIS2 simplification amendments")
- A technical disclosure (e.g., "Google 2026 research: ECC breakable at ~1,200 logical qubits")

**What does NOT qualify:**
- "Experts say..." (who? what experts? cite them or don't use it)
- "There is growing concern..." (vague, unfalsifiable)
- "It is widely believed..." (by whom?)
- Any claim without a traceable source

### Step 2: Structural Analysis
The forces, incentives, and constraints that make the trajectory legible. This is where the analytical work happens.

**Ask these structural questions:**
- **Who benefits?** Follow the incentives, not the rhetoric
- **What are the structural constraints?** Geography, economics, demography, physics — these don't change with elections
- **What is the feedback loop?** Does this force amplify itself or create a countervailing reaction?
- **Where is the chokepoint?** Every system has a node where control is concentrated
- **What is the timeline of deployment vs. governance?** Technology always deploys faster than regulation
- **Who is the weakest link?** Systems fail at their least-defended point, not their most-defended
- **What happened last time?** Historical precedent constrains the range of plausible outcomes

**Analytical frameworks to apply:**
- **Chokepoint analysis**: Identify where control concentrates and who holds it
- **Deployment-governance gap**: Technology is deployed 5-10 years before governance catches up — always
- **Interdependence weaponization**: Every node of globalization is simultaneously a cooperative link and a coercive lever
- **Structural fragmentation**: Systems under stress fragment along pre-existing fault lines
- **Pre-positioning doctrine**: The most dangerous capability is the one deployed before the conflict begins
- **Cascading failure**: Infrastructure planned as discrete assets behaves as interconnected networks under stress
- **Demographic trajectory**: Population decline in Western nations, East Asia, Russia, and China constrains manpower-dependent strategies and incentivizes escalation to decisive technologies (autonomous weapons, cyber, nuclear)
- **Fork-in-the-road monitoring**: The scenarios excluded because they're too catastrophic (nuclear war, civilizational fragmentation) must be actively monitored, not assumed away. The forces converging toward nuclear escalation — doctrine changes, autonomous weapons deployment, governance rejection, demographic decline — are observable now and must be tracked, not excluded

### Step 3: Projection
Where the forces lead if no countervailing action intervenes. Stated as conditional, not certain. Always time-bounded.

**Projection rules:**
- State projections as conditional: "If no countervailing force intervenes, by [year]..."
- Use specific time horizons: 2028 (near-term), 2030 (medium), 2035 (structural), 2040 (generational)
- Never project beyond what the structural forces support
- Name the countervailing force that could prevent the outcome
- The projection should feel inevitable once the structural analysis is understood
- End with a reframing — the reader should see the present differently after reading the projection

## Output Format: The Predictive Assessment

Each assessment should follow this template:

```
### [Number]. "[Title]"
**[One-sentence thesis in bold — the headline that would make a senior intelligence analyst stop scrolling.]**

[2-3 paragraphs of flowing prose. No bullet points in the body. The first paragraph grounds in verified data. The second paragraph performs structural analysis. The third paragraph projects forward with a specific time horizon.]

- **Grounding data**: [Specific facts with dates]
- **Sources**: [Named sources — publications, agencies, researchers]
- **Brand**: [DSI | GISI | ARIA]
- **Projection horizon**: [Year]
- **Target outlets**: [2-3 publication targets]
```

## Brand Routing

### DSI (Digital Security Insights) — Cyan
**Domain**: Technical security, infrastructure vulnerability, cyber operations, OT/ICS security
**Voice**: Authoritative, analytical, urgent but measured
**Hook style**: "Imagine..." openings that place the reader inside the problem
**Key devices**: Simile (concrete comparisons), paradox framing (efficiency creates fragility)
**Signature move**: Making invisible infrastructure visible
**Categories**: Nation-State Threats, Cybersecurity, Critical Infrastructure, Digital Identity, Risk Intelligence

### GISI (Global Intelligence & Strategic Insights) — Amber
**Domain**: Geopolitics, power dynamics, chokepoints, economic warfare, Global South agency
**Voice**: Contrarian, bold, unapologetic, intellectually rigorous
**Hook style**: Provocative opening question or historical parallel
**Key devices**: Extended analogy (carries the entire argument), Franz Fanon moral anchor, historical parallels
**Signature move**: Turning the powerful's own words against their narrative
**Categories**: Strategic Analysis, Chokepoint Doctrine, Regional Assessment, Power Dynamics

### ARIA (AI Risk & Intelligence Advisory) — Violet
**Domain**: AI governance, autonomous weapons, model risk, training data integrity, AI-enabled threats
**Voice**: Practitioner-grade, research-grounded, incident-driven
**Hook style**: A specific AI incident or deployment fact that reveals a structural vulnerability
**Key devices**: Paradox framing (the solution creates the problem), dependency analysis
**Signature move**: Showing that AI risk is a cybersecurity/geopolitics problem, not just an ethics problem
**Categories**: AI Governance, AI Safety, Autonomous Systems, Model Risk, AI-Enabled Threats

## Domain Watchlist — Where to Look for Grounding Data

### Cyber/Technical (DSI)
- CISA/US-CERT advisories
- Mandiant, CrowdStrike, Recorded Future, Dragos threat reports
- ESET, Check Point, Unit42, Symantec research
- CERT-UA, ENISA advisories
- Zero-day disclosures (Google TAG, Microsoft MSRC)
- OT/ICS incident reports (Industrial Cyber, SecurityWeek)

### Geopolitical (GISI)
- CFR, Carnegie Endowment, CSIS, Brookings, Chatham House
- RUSI, ASPI (The Strategist), Lowy Institute
- Atlantic Council, New Lines Institute
- Reuters, Al Jazeera, AFP for event grounding
- BRICS/AU/ASEAN communiques and policy documents
- UN voting records and treaty negotiations

### AI Risk (ARIA)
- NIST AI frameworks and standards
- EU AI Act enforcement notices
- AI incident databases (AIAAIC)
- Company disclosures (AI transparency reports, safety frameworks)
- Defense procurement documents (Pentagon AI budget, Replicator program)
- Academic papers (arXiv, NeurIPS, ICML for capability benchmarks)

### Cross-Domain (Critical)
- WEF Global Risks Report (annual)
- US IC Annual Threat Assessment
- ODNI worldwide threat briefings
- Deloitte/McKinsey/Gartner industry forecasts (for market sizing only)
- Patent filings (for capability trajectory)
- Job postings (for organizational intent — "what they're hiring for tells you what they're building")

## Research Protocol

Before generating assessments, follow this sequence:

1. **Scan current events** (web search for each domain area — last 90 days)
2. **Identify structural shifts** — not headlines, but the forces beneath them
3. **Verify grounding data** — every fact must have a named source
4. **Apply structural analysis** — use the analytical frameworks above
5. **Project forward** — conditional, time-bounded, with countervailing forces named
6. **Route to brand** — DSI for technical, GISI for geopolitical, ARIA for AI risk
7. **Cross-reference existing coverage** — check `work_products/dsi-cyber-ops-article-topics.md` and `work_products/dsi-predictive-intelligence-25-ideas.md` to avoid duplication

## DSI's Analytical Axioms

Derived from studying the track record of structural forecasting over three decades:

1. **Technology trajectories are ~90% predictable** from R&D observation and deployment patterns. If the research exists and the incentive structure supports deployment, the technology will arrive — often ahead of schedule.
2. **Institutional trajectories are unreliable.** Whether institutions strengthen or weaken is politically contingent, not structurally determined. Never build a projection on the assumption that governance will catch up.
3. **Quality-of-life projections fail** because they require assumptions about political will, not just capability. Capability is predictable. Will is not.
4. **Demographic forces are the slowest and most reliable.** Birth rates, aging, and population decline are observable decades in advance and nearly impossible to reverse once established. They constrain everything — military strategy, economic growth, social stability.
5. **The catastrophic scenarios you exclude are the ones you should monitor most carefully.** Nuclear escalation, civilizational fragmentation, systemic infrastructure collapse — analysts routinely exclude these because they invalidate all other projections. But the forces converging toward them are observable: doctrine changes, autonomous weapons deployment, governance rejection by nuclear powers, demographic decline removing the manpower alternative to escalation. Monitor the convergence. Don't assume it away.

## Anti-Patterns — What This Skill Does NOT Do

- **No punditry**: "I think X will happen" is not analysis. Structural forces are.
- **No ideology**: The skill doesn't argue for outcomes. It identifies trajectories.
- **No hedging disguised as analysis**: "It remains to be seen" is not a projection. Name the forces and their direction.
- **No trend extrapolation without structural support**: "X increased 20% last year, so it will increase 20% next year" is not analysis. Why did it increase? What forces drove it? Are those forces accelerating or decelerating?
- **No vague timelines**: "In the coming years" is meaningless. State a year.
- **No unsourced claims**: If you can't cite it, you can't use it.
- **No moral conclusions**: The skill identifies what WILL happen, not what SHOULD happen. The reader draws their own conclusions.

1. Research all 15 domain areas simultaneously (use parallel web searches)
